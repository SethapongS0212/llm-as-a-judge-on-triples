Our prediction approach with the replacing SMOTE with SMOT-EEN resulted in an accuracy of 0.95, which is close to the accuracy of the original experiment using SMOTE. Therefore, the results suggest that the data synthesis was not negatively impacted by noise presented in the processed data. In addition, the dataset was first divided into training and testing sets prior to oversampling, ensuring that SMOTE was applied to the training data and thus avoiding any data leakage into the test set. This design maintains the validity of performance evaluation. Thus, the application of SMOTE under these controlled conditions provides an advantage in enhancing model generalization in small or imbalanced datasets while minimizing the risk of overfitting.
